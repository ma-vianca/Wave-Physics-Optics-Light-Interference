Wave Optics & Refraction Theory

Light exhibits both wave-like and particle-like properties. Phenomena such as interference, diffraction, and refraction provide conclusive proof of the wave nature of light.

Young's Double-Slit Experiment

When coherent light passes through two parallel narrow slits separated by distance $d$, spherical wavelets overlap, creating bright (constructive) and dark (destructive) interference fringes on a screen.

Constructive (Bright): $d \sin\theta = m \lambda$
Destructive (Dark): $d \sin\theta = (m + \frac{1}{2}) \lambda$
Fringe Width: $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$
Refraction & Prism Dispersion

When light crosses an interface into a medium of different optical density, its speed changes, bending the ray according to Snell's Law. Shorter wavelengths (violet) bend more than longer wavelengths (red).

Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$
Prism Deviation ($\delta$): $\delta = i_1 + i_2 - A$
Refractive Index: $n(\lambda) = \frac{c}{v(\lambda)}$

Key Mathematical Equations

Double Slit Intensity
$$I(\theta) = I_0 \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi d \sin\theta}{\lambda}\right) \cdot \operatorname{sinc}^2\left(\frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda}\right)$$

Combines double-slit interference with single-slit diffraction envelope.

Cauchy's Dispersion Equation
$$n(\lambda) = A + \frac{B}{\lambda^2} + \frac{C}{\lambda^4}$$

Models how the refractive index $n$ increases for shorter wavelengths $\lambda$.

Minimum Prism Deviation
$$n = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + \delta_{min}}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}$$

Relates prism apex angle $A$ and minimum deviation angle $\delta_{min}$ to material index $n$.

Concept #1

What happens to Young's fringe spacing when slit distance ($d$) is halved?

Click to flip
Explanation

Fringe spacing doubles ($\Delta y \propto \frac{1}{d}$).
Since $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$, reducing slit separation forces light waves to diverge at steeper angles to produce constructive interference.
